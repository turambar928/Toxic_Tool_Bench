Tool-augmented data agents can execute a tool successfully yet reach a wrong conclusion from a corrupted response. ToxicBench studies this gap through paired clean and poisoned observations with fixed source data. Its diagnostic tasks cover numerical, label, schema, and retrieval errors. Trajectory metrics distinguish requesting a check from adopting the correct answer: repeated poisoning produces adoption after checking, corroborated by human annotations. Two-route controls compare ordinary retries with explicit verification under the same step and per-request token caps, while field-bound experiments on three public tables isolate the effect of supplied evidence. A post-freeze human evaluation of 200 trajectories yields 96\% task-success scoring agreement and supports both the retry benefit over Base on the audited tasks and poisoned-answer adoption after checking. We release versioned scoring, reference and delivery audits alongside the trajectories. Together, these results make evidence availability and final-answer selection explicit dimensions of evaluating tool-augmented data agents.
