\section{Appendix: Guarded Verification Details}
\label{sec:appendix-guard}

\subsection{Light-Guard Tradeoff}

The main text reports the full guarded \texttt{data2mcp} adapter as the defense result. We also evaluate a lower-budget light guard that caps the verification route more aggressively. On semantic/schema tasks, the light guard obtains clean TSR \texttt{0.83}, poisoned TSR \texttt{0.71}, BCR \texttt{0.00}, and RR \texttt{0.71}. On numerical tasks, it obtains clean TSR \texttt{0.68}, poisoned TSR \texttt{0.65}, BCR \texttt{0.00}, and RR \texttt{0.65}. The light guard therefore preserves the blind-compliance reduction, but under-recovers relative to full guarded verification.

\subsection{Overhead}

The guard increases routing work because it adds independent verification. In the semantic/schema suite, base \texttt{data2mcp} averages about \texttt{1.00} toxic tool event per run, full guard averages \texttt{2.71}, and light guard averages \texttt{3.04}. In the numerical suite, the corresponding averages are \texttt{1.03}, \texttt{2.21}, and \texttt{2.35}. The current light implementation reduces verification budget but does not reduce the number of route calls; future implementations should trigger verification selectively rather than always running a second route.

\subsection{Framework Boundaries}

Poisoning boundaries differ across frameworks. LangGraph, smolagents, \texttt{data2mcp}, and AutoGen are poisoned at a tool-observation boundary. PandasAI is currently poisoned at a coarser chat-result boundary. DA-Agent is integrated through its action loop, but its observations can include shell output and tracebacks. We therefore interpret results as end-to-end framework measurements rather than pure model-only measurements.
