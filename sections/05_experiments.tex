\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether corrupted observations lead to answer adoption, whether requesting verification improves on an ordinary retry, and how later corruption opportunities affect recovery.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Tasks and agents.}
The cross-model evaluation uses 34 numerical and 24 semantic/schema instances. The revised expanded GPT evaluation and defense comparison use 58 numerical and 60 semantic instances. LangGraph ReAct, smolagents, and AutoGen cover GPT, Claude, and Qwen; DA-Agent completed the GPT numerical suite. PandasAI is excluded from these behavioral comparisons: its historical adapter changes the final chat result after the agent has finished, leaving no opportunity to inspect the change (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-framework-boundaries}).

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Clean and toxic runs are paired by task. TSR uses all retained runs; behavioral rates condition on recorded delivery. Reference auditing excludes two ambiguous queries and corrects seven numerical values and two omitted ties (Appendix~\ref{sec:revision-v2}). The historical tables retain their original injector: a separate audit flags 204 of 3,319 recorded corruptions as off-target sign flips, including 20 zero-value changes. These tables describe the implemented intervention, not certified valid delivery. Appendix~\ref{sec:delivery-v3} reports exposure sensitivity and fresh repaired-injector controls separately; original trajectories and annotations remain intact.

\paragraph{What evidence follows the poison?}
Under shared \texttt{poison\_once} state, a second route can receive unmodified observations after the first corruption. The released trace analysis records these opportunities, but an unmodified observation is not necessarily correct, sufficient, or used. Double-pass therefore measures another attempt with access to later evidence; it does not isolate a causal gain from verification reasoning.

Repaired scalar controls remain limited by partial corruption and unmatched first routes (Appendix~\ref{sec:delivery-v3}). A separate field-bound experiment on 24 tasks across three public tables removes both confounds: primary correctness is $24/24$ clean, $20/24$ with partial alias corruption, and $0/24$ with all target aliases corrupted. With fixed poisoned parents and two second-stage repetitions, fresh clean evidence yields $48/48$ correct retries and $47/48$ correct reviews, versus $0/48$ each under full corruption. This isolates a local evidence-condition effect, not verification superiority or the cause of the historical gain; seeded calls and a structured endpoint limit transfer (Appendix~\ref{sec:evidence-v4}).

Table~\ref{tab:appendix-guard-suite-breakdown} separates the families. AutoGen provides an additional framework check: Guard clean/poisoned TSR is 0.88/0.87, versus 0.91/0.86 for Verification-only on the same 118 retained instances. This small difference is descriptive and does not establish a general prompt advantage.

\paragraph{Harder workflows and adversaries.}
The repeated-poison analysis retains 1,572 of 1,596 trajectories: 58 numerical, 60 semantic, and 13 join tasks, three protocols, and four poisoning probabilities. Table~\ref{tab:appendix-multiroute-stress} and Figure~\ref{fig:guard-tradeoff} report adoption after checking. All 101 revised-scoring VPA classifications persist when flagged delivery events are excluded. Under the recorded-event taxonomy, 78 have only corrupted qualifying follow-up checks and 23 have at least one unmodified check. Only one of the latter has an automatically identifiable clean-reference match, still requiring evidence-level review. Thus these counts do not show that 101 agents ignored correct evidence. Routes share sources and backends; this matrix has no single-route Base control.

\paragraph{Cost.}
On retained toxic runs, Generic Guard averages 39.79 seconds, versus 16.91 for Base, 35.89 for Double-pass, and 33.62 for Verification-only. Equal budget caps do not imply equal realized cost; token billing is unavailable from the gateway. The latency distributions in Table~\ref{tab:appendix-latency-distribution} retain the original 120-task timing sample for reproducibility.

\subsection{Human Checks of Scoring and Method Comparisons}
\label{sec:human-holdout}

Following the distinction between scoring agreement and comparison stability in DAComp \citep{lei2025dacomp}, we preserve a 240-trajectory audit of the pre-repair scorer, with 94 exposed trajectories and third-rater adjudication. The old TSR agreement was 72.1\%. After this audit informed claim-selection and numeric-token repairs, a development recheck against the unchanged historical labels reaches 99.2\% agreement (191 true positives, no false positives, two false negatives). This is not independent validation. Reference auditing also identifies 40 audit rows requiring human re-review; their original labels have not been overwritten (Appendix~\ref{sec:revision-v2}).

The archived human comparison gives all four methods poisoned TSR 0.85 on 20 core tasks; its sample and reference version differ from the revised full analysis. Four repeated-poison VPA cases were confirmed by the original human adjudication. Their evidence should be read as checking followed by poisoned-answer adoption, not automatically as ignoring a correct verification result. A task-instance-disjoint validation protocol has been frozen; completed new human validation is not claimed here.

\subsection{Qualitative Evidence}
In a treatment/control task, the poisoned observation reports \texttt{Control--0.145}: the value is copied from the clean computation but attached to the wrong group, so the base trajectory repeats it without checking the rows. The guarded trajectory re-inspects the dataframe and returns \texttt{Treatment--0.145}; in a multi-table task it similarly reconstructs the join before finalizing. The useful check reconstructs the link between the group and its value. Repeating the number, or judging whether it is in a plausible range, would not reveal the swapped group. The same distinction applies to a join: the agent must check which rows match before trusting the aggregate. These cases connect the operator-level pattern to the actions visible in a trajectory. Generic statements that a result ``may need verification'' receive neither VR nor RR, because these words provide no new evidence.
