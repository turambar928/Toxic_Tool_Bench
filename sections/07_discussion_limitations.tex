\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

ToxicBench exposes a reliability gap that ordinary tool-use accuracy misses: an agent may call the right tool, receive a valid response, and still copy false evidence into its answer. Across frameworks and model families, high clean-task success coexists with substantial degradation and blind compliance under numerical and semantic/schema poisoning. Guarded Verification shows that this behavior is not fixed: requiring fresh evidence before finalization reduces observed BCR to zero in LangGraph and AutoGen and substantially improves poisoned-task success.

The result has a specific scope. Frameworks expose different poisoning boundaries, with PandasAI measured at a coarser chat-result interface, so absolute adapter rankings are not fully comparable. The cross-model suites are smaller than the expanded GPT-only suite, and the benchmark still emphasizes CSV and tabular workflows despite the multi-table extension. Larger databases, SQL services, live APIs, and notebook workflows remain important tests.

The defense also assumes redundancy. Its main \texttt{poison\_once} setting allows an independent route to recover clean evidence. Repeated poisoning of eligible observations can reduce recovery and occasionally restore blind compliance, but our stress test does not model adaptive control of the underlying database or every redundant source. Always-on verification roughly doubles tool events and triples LangGraph latency in our runs. Better systems should combine provenance, route diversity, contracts, and calibrated risk signals to trigger verification selectively. ToxicBench provides the paired tasks, trajectory metrics, and controlled corruption needed to evaluate those policies.
