Tool-augmented data agents can execute a tool successfully yet reach a wrong conclusion from a corrupted response. ToxicBench studies this gap through paired clean and poisoned observations with fixed source data. Its diagnostic tasks cover numerical, label, schema, and retrieval errors. Trajectory metrics distinguish requesting a check from adopting the correct answer: repeated poisoning produces adoption after checking, including four human-confirmed cases. Two-route controls compare ordinary retries with explicit verification under the same step and per-request token caps. These comparisons do not establish a general advantage for verification prompts; their interpretation depends on which observations remain uncorrupted. We separately audit answer scoring, reference answers, and actual corruption delivery, preserving historical results and isolating repaired-injector experiments. The resulting benchmark supports inspectable studies of evidence availability and answer selection, rather than claims of a general-purpose defense or deployment-level robustness.
