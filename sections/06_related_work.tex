\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Tool use, reliability, and poisoned evidence.}
Tool-augmented language models interleave reasoning with API calls and program execution \citep{schick2023toolformer,yao2023react,gao2023pal}. Benchmarks such as ToolBench, API-Bank, Gorilla, and broader agent suites largely stress tool selection, argument construction, planning, and recovery from explicit failures \citep{qin2023toolllm,li2023apibank,patil2024gorilla,liu2023agentbench,zhou2023webarena,vuddanti2025paladin}, while trajectory-focused analyses argue for decomposing reliability beyond end-task success \citep{mazaheri2026agentatlas,vuddanti2026err,kohl2026structural}. In parallel, security work shows that agents can be misled by malicious instructions/metadata (prompt injection, tool-description attacks) or by corrupted tool outputs and retrieved contexts \citep{perez2022ignore,greshake2023indirect,li2026mcpitp,ye2026trustdesc,li2026agentcanary,wu2026agentdrift,zhang2026agentsentry,liang2025saferag,chen2025poisonarena,li2025cparag}. ToxicBench targets a distinct failure point: the correct tool is executed and returns \emph{success}, but the observation is factually or semantically corrupted, shifting the problem from tool selection to evidence trust.

\paragraph{Closest work and positioning.}
Closest efforts differ along five dimensions: attack position, task domain, error type, metric granularity, and defense mechanism. \emph{Tools Fail} studies detection and recovery from silent tool errors, but does not provide ToxicBench's paired data-analysis suites or its decomposition into blind compliance, anomaly detection, validation, and recovery \citep{sun2024toolsfail}. ToolBench-X considers broader unreliable environments (e.g., output drift), whereas ToxicBench specializes in successful yet task-relevant numerical and semantic corruption in data-analysis workflows \citep{tian2026toolbenchx}. AgentDojo emphasizes prompt-injection defenses in dynamic tool environments \citep{debenedetti2024agentdojo}, and PoisonedRAG corrupts a retrieval corpus \citep{zou2024poisonedrag}; ToxicBench instead standardizes post-execution corruption at the agent--tool observation boundary, evaluates temporally ordered behavior rather than only outcomes, and uses evidence collection as a controlled defense factor. Thus, our claim is not that silent tool errors are a new failure family in general, but that this benchmark makes their data-analysis observation-boundary manifestation and trust-calibration consequences measurable.

\paragraph{Data quality and verification defenses.}
Data-validation and table-reasoning benchmarks address imperfect inputs and schema/quality issues \citep{schelter2018automating,breck2019data,gu2025radar}. Existing defenses filter or constrain evidence via provenance/credibility ranking, sanitization, runtime policies, output contracts, or claim checking \citep{dekel2026sdag,jia2025ragrank,patil2026ragshield,hamad2025toolcritic}. These are adjacent but do not directly provide a drop-in baseline for arbitrary dataframe/schema observations under silent corruption. Guarded Verification instead enforces a fresh evidence-producing action at the observation boundary; it relates to self-refinement and metacognitive verification \citep{shinn2023reflexion,madaan2023selfrefine,zhou2024metarag,zhang2025cosight,ma2025pou}, and can be combined with confidence-aware triggering and calibration methods \citep{xuan2026confidence,wang2026mirror,subramani2025mice,gurram2026agentprop}.
